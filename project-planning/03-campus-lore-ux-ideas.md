# CampusLore Brand & UX Guidelines
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Draft  
**Last Updated:** August 2026

---

# 1. Introduction

## What is CampusLore?

CampusLore is a private, university-focused knowledge-sharing platform where verified students document experiences, preserve institutional knowledge, and help future batches make informed decisions.

Unlike traditional social platforms that optimize for engagement, CampusLore optimizes for **usefulness**.

It is a place where every post should have lasting value.

Examples include:

- Placement experiences
- Internship preparation
- Academic advice
- Hostel reviews
- Faculty insights
- Club experiences
- Research guidance
- Campus resources

The platform aims to become the digital knowledge archive of a university.

---

# 2. Vision

> **To preserve and pass down campus knowledge from one generation of students to the next.**

Every graduating batch carries years of valuable experience.

CampusLore exists to ensure that knowledge is never lost.

Instead of every student solving the same problems repeatedly, students should be able to learn from those who came before them.

---

# 3. Mission

To build the most trusted student knowledge platform by combining:

- Verified identities
- Authentic experiences
- Community collaboration
- Long-term knowledge preservation

---

# 4. Product Positioning

CampusLore is **not** another social media platform.

It is closer to:

- A university knowledge base
- A student community
- A campus operating system

than Instagram, Facebook, or Reddit.

## Positioning Statement

> **CampusLore helps verified university students discover trusted campus knowledge through real experiences shared by seniors and peers.**

---

# 5. Core Principles

Every feature introduced into CampusLore should support one or more of the following principles.

## 5.1 Knowledge Before Popularity

The platform values information quality over engagement metrics.

Instead of promoting vanity metrics, CampusLore rewards usefulness.

Instead of:

❤️ Likes

CampusLore uses:

✅ Helpful

The goal is to encourage students to contribute meaningful knowledge rather than chase popularity.

---

## 5.2 Community Before Algorithms

CampusLore should recommend content primarily based on:

- Relevance
- Academic context
- Categories
- Student interests

The platform should avoid addictive engagement algorithms.

---

## 5.3 Readability First

CampusLore is primarily a reading platform.

Students spend significantly more time consuming knowledge than creating it.

Therefore every design decision should prioritize:

- Typography
- Spacing
- Contrast
- Comfortable reading width
- Minimal distractions

---

## 5.4 Calm Interface

CampusLore should never feel overwhelming.

Avoid:

- Excessive animations
- Loud colors
- Flashy gradients
- Notification overload
- Visual clutter

The interface should reduce cognitive load.

---

## 5.5 Long-Term Value

Experiences should remain valuable months or even years after publication.

CampusLore encourages timeless knowledge instead of temporary updates.

---

## 5.6 Authenticity

Every contributor is a verified student.

Trust comes from knowing that experiences are written by genuine members of the institution.

---

- Helpful
- Knowledgeable
- Professional
- Friendly
- Curious
- Reliable
- Organized
- Approachable

- Flashy
- Chaotic

# 9. Information Hierarchy

Every page should answer these questions in order.

## Level 1 — Where am I?

Navigation should always make the user's current location obvious.

---

## Level 2 — What matters most?

Primary content should receive the greatest visual emphasis.

---

## Level 3 — What else is happening?

Campus Pulse provides supporting information without distracting from the main content.

---

## Level 4 — What can I do next?

Every page should clearly guide users toward the next meaningful action.

---

# 10. Navigation Philosophy

CampusLore divides navigation into three distinct regions.

## Left Sidebar — Actions

Contains primary navigation.

Examples:

- Home
- Explore
- Write Story
- Mentorship
- Bookmarks
- My Journey
- Settings

---

## Main Content — Knowledge

Contains the user's primary task.

Examples:

- Stories
- Discussions
- Profiles
- Search results
- Comments

---

## Right Sidebar — Campus Pulse

Contains live campus information.

It should **never** function as navigation.

Possible widgets include:

- Upcoming Events
- Trending Topics
- Mentor Spotlight
- Featured Story
- Announcements
- Placement Calendar
- Exam Schedule
- Student Achievements

This separation should remain consistent across the application.

---

# 11. Campus Pulse

Campus Pulse is one of CampusLore's defining experiences.

Rather than displaying random widgets, Campus Pulse answers one simple question:

> **"What is happening on campus right now?"**

Possible widgets include:

- Upcoming Hackathons
- Technical Workshops
- Cultural Events
- Placement Drives
- Department Seminars
- Exam Schedule
- Trending Discussions
- Featured Stories
- Student Achievements

Campus Pulse should make the platform feel alive.

---

# 12. Product Language

Words define the brand experience.

CampusLore intentionally avoids generic terminology.

| Generic | CampusLore |
|----------|------------|
| Dashboard | Home |
| Feed | Campus Feed |
| Post | Story / Experience |
| User | Student |
| Profile | My Journey |
| Categories | Campus Topics |
| Trending | Campus Pulse |
| Notification | Activity |
| Like | Helpful |
| Saved | Bookmarks |

All interface copy should consistently follow this vocabulary.

---

# 13. Content Philosophy

Content should be:

- Evergreen
- Honest
- Practical
- Experience-driven
- Actionable
- Helpful

Encourage stories like:

> "Here's what I learned during my internship interview."

instead of

> "College is fun."

The objective is preserving institutional knowledge.

---

# 14. Community Philosophy

CampusLore rewards contribution rather than popularity.

Recognition comes from:

- Helpful stories
- Detailed comments
- Mentorship
- Community participation
- Knowledge sharing

Students should feel motivated by impact rather than attention.

---

# 15. User Experience Principles

Every interaction should feel:

- Predictable
- Fast
- Accessible
- Readable
- Forgiving

Examples include:

- Never lose user input unexpectedly.
- Confirm destructive actions.
- Display meaningful loading states.
- Use skeleton loaders where appropriate.
- Provide informative empty states.
- Explain errors in plain language.
- Offer immediate feedback after user actions.

---

# 16. Accessibility Philosophy

CampusLore should be usable by everyone.

Guidelines:

- Maintain WCAG-compliant color contrast.
- Support keyboard navigation.
- Provide visible focus states.
- Respect reduced motion preferences.
- Ensure readable typography.
- Use semantic HTML whenever possible.
- Design touch-friendly interactions for mobile devices.

Accessibility is a core design requirement rather than an afterthought.

---

# 17. Future Vision

CampusLore should gradually evolve into the central digital platform students open every day.

Future capabilities include:

- AI-assisted knowledge discovery
- Personalized recommendations
- Campus-wide event management
- Alumni mentorship
- University-wide analytics
- Multi-university support

Every future feature should strengthen CampusLore's identity rather than redefine it.

---

# 18. Guiding Principle

Every design, engineering, and product decision should support the following statement:

> **CampusLore is not a social media platform. It is the trusted knowledge and community hub for a university, where every interaction should leave a student more informed, more connected, or better prepared than before.**

When evaluating any future feature, ask:

- Does it help students **learn**?
- Does it help students **connect**?
- Does it help students **stay informed**?
- Does it help students **grow**?

If the answer is **no**, the feature likely does not belong in CampusLore.
